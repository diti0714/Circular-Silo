# 1. Game Concept

**Circular Silo** is a single-player sorting, resource-management, repair, and puzzle game set in a post-apocalyptic underground society.

After a technological war causes catastrophic environmental damage to the Earth's surface, humanity survives inside underground structures known as Silos. Resources inside the Silo are limited, meaning that broken or unwanted objects cannot simply be replaced with new ones.

The player joins the Silo's Mechanical department as an inexperienced worker responsible for processing objects recovered by scavenging teams. Each object must be inspected and assigned an appropriate destination. Depending on its condition, an object may be reused, repaired, dismantled for materials, recycled, or discarded.

Objects that appear to be repairable can be sent to **Martha Walker's workshop**, where the player learns how to diagnose and repair different objects through interactive repair puzzles.

As the player progresses, they gain access to an old-world **quantum diagnostic device**. The device uses quantum measurement to determine the state of certain advanced electronic components. The player must use the measurement result to decide whether an object should be repaired, recycled, or discarded.

The game introduces the principles of **reuse, repair, recycling, resource conservation, circular economy, and basic quantum measurement** through gameplay.

# 2. Story / Theme

In the future, a technological war between nations leads to the development of advanced nanotechnology controlled by artificial intelligence. The technology is inexpensive and can be produced on a massive scale, but its uncontrolled use causes catastrophic environmental damage.

The Earth's surface becomes contaminated with toxic substances, making the air unsafe for humans to breathe. With the surface no longer suitable for human life, the remaining population moves underground into enormous shelters known as **Silos**.

Life inside the Silo is highly dependent on limited resources. New products and replacement parts cannot simply be produced whenever something breaks. Every material and manufactured object therefore has value, and unnecessary waste can directly affect the survival of the community.

Objects recovered by scavenging teams are brought to the Mechanical department. Workers examine and sort these objects to determine what should happen to them. Some objects can be reused immediately, some can be dismantled so that their materials can be recovered, and some can be repaired and returned to use.

The player joins the Mechanical department as an inexperienced worker and learns how the Silo handles discarded objects.

**Martha Walker**, an experienced mechanic who runs an electronics workshop, becomes the player's mentor. She teaches the player how to identify objects that are worth repairing, how to diagnose simple faults, and how to extend the useful life of existing products instead of immediately replacing them.

As the player progresses, they discover that the Silo still possesses fragments of advanced technology from before the collapse. Among these technologies is a quantum diagnostic device that can be used to determine the state of certain electronic components through quantum measurement.

The player gradually learns to combine traditional mechanical knowledge with this technology to make better decisions about whether objects should be repaired, reused, recycled, or discarded.

# 3. Social Good Theme

The game addresses **sustainable consumption and production**, particularly:

* reducing unnecessary waste;
* extending the useful life of products;
* repairing instead of replacing;
* reusing existing objects;
* recovering useful materials through recycling;
* making responsible decisions when resources are limited.

The primary UN Sustainable Development Goal connected to the game is:

## SDG 12: Responsible Consumption and Production

SDG 12 aims to ensure sustainable consumption and production patterns.

The game explores this goal through several concepts:

* **Reuse:** Using an existing object again instead of replacing it.
* **Repair:** Extending the useful lifetime of broken objects.
* **Recycling:** Recovering valuable materials from objects that cannot be reused or repaired.
* **Resource conservation:** Making decisions when repair resources and materials are limited.
* **Circular economy:** Keeping products and materials in use for as long as possible instead of following a linear "produce → use → discard" model.

The issue was chosen because modern societies consume large amounts of products and materials, while many discarded objects still contain useful components or can potentially be repaired.

Greater awareness of responsible consumption and production could benefit individuals, communities, repair industries, recycling systems, manufacturers, and the environment.

# 4. Social Good Integration into Gameplay

The social-good theme is directly incorporated into the game's core mechanics.

The player does not simply read information about sustainability. Every object encountered requires the player to decide how its useful life should be handled.

For each object, the player must determine whether it should be:

**Reused → Repaired → Recycled → Discarded**

depending on its condition.

For example, the player may receive a broken electronic device.

Possible choices could include:

* sending it directly to waste;
* recycling its materials;
* sending it to Martha's workshop for repair.

If the player correctly identifies an object as repairable and successfully repairs it, the object returns to use instead of becoming waste.

Later levels introduce limited repair resources, preventing the player from simply choosing "repair" for every object. The player must consider whether repairing a particular object is worth the resources required.

The game therefore turns sustainable consumption into a decision-making mechanic.

# 5. Quantum Contribution

The game introduces a simple quantum mechanic through **quantum measurement and quantum randomness**.

Certain advanced electronic components recovered by the scavenging teams have an unknown state. Before measurement, the player does not know whether the component is functioning or damaged.

The Silo has an old quantum diagnostic device that can measure the component's state.

Conceptually, the device represents a qubit that can produce one of two measurement outcomes:

* **0 → Component functioning**
* **1 → Component damaged**

The player activates the diagnostic device and receives a measurement result. They must then use that information to decide what to do with the object.

For example:

```text
        Electronic Component
                  ↓
          Unknown State
                  ↓
        Quantum Measurement
              ↙       ↘
        Functioning   Damaged
            ↓            ↓
          Reuse       Repair / Recycle
```

The quantum mechanic introduces the idea that a quantum system can have an uncertain state before measurement and that measurement produces a definite outcome.

The quantum mechanic is deliberately kept small so that it supports the main game rather than overwhelming the sustainability and repair mechanics.

# 6. Intended Impact / Message

The main message of the game is:

> **Before something becomes waste, consider whether it can still be used, repaired, or recovered.**

Players should develop a basic understanding of:

* the difference between reuse, repair, recycling, and disposal;
* why extending product lifetimes can reduce waste;
* how resource scarcity affects decision-making;
* the basic principles of a circular economy;
* how repairing an existing object can sometimes be preferable to replacing it;
* the basic concept of quantum measurement through an interactive game mechanic.

The game should encourage players to question the assumption that a broken object has automatically reached the end of its useful life.

### Measuring the Intended Impact

The game's message could be evaluated through a short questionnaire before and after playing.

Players could answer questions about how they would deal with broken or unwanted products before playing the game and then answer similar questions afterward.

The responses could be compared to determine whether the game improved players' understanding of reuse, repair, recycling, and responsible consumption.

For the quantum component, players could also be asked whether they understood that the diagnostic device produces a definite result through measurement after initially representing an uncertain state.

# 7. Gameplay and Rules

## Main Player Actions

The player will:

1. Receive objects recovered by scavenging teams.
2. Inspect the objects.
3. Determine their condition.
4. Sort them into the appropriate category.
5. Decide whether an object should be reused, repaired, recycled, or discarded.
6. Send repairable objects to Martha's workshop.
7. Follow Martha's repair instructions.
8. Identify the correct components or actions during repair.
9. Use the quantum diagnostic device when required.
10. Make decisions based on the measurement result.
11. Earn or lose points based on their decisions.
12. Progress through increasingly difficult levels.

---

## Game Structure

The game is **single-player**.

The initial version will contain approximately **five levels**, with each level introducing an additional mechanic or increasing the difficulty.

### Level 1: Basic Sorting

The player learns the basic categories:

* Reuse
* Repair
* Recycle
* Waste

The objects are relatively simple and the player receives feedback after each decision.

---

### Level 2: Identifying Repairable Objects

The player must distinguish between objects that can realistically be repaired and objects that should instead be recycled or discarded.

Objects contain clues about their condition that the player needs to inspect.

---

### Level 3: Martha's Workshop

The player begins repairing objects.

Martha provides instructions such as:

> "Check the power connection."

The player must identify the relevant component on the object.

**Correct selection:** Positive points

**Incorrect selection:** Negative points

Successful completion returns the object to usable condition.

---

### Level 4: Quantum Diagnostics

The player gains access to the Silo's quantum diagnostic device.

Some advanced electronic objects have an unknown state.

The player can perform a quantum measurement to determine their state.

For example:

```text
Unknown electronic component
             ↓
      Quantum measurement
             ↓
      ┌──────┴──────┐
      ↓             ↓
 Functioning      Damaged
      ↓             ↓
   Reuse        Repair / Recycle
```

The player then makes the appropriate decision based on the measurement result.

This introduces the basic concept of quantum measurement and quantum randomness into the game.

---

### Level 5: Circular Resource Management

The player manages a larger and more complex flow of objects.

They must balance:

**Repair + Reuse + Material Recovery + Waste**

The player has limited repair resources and must decide which objects are worth repairing.

Quantum diagnostics may also be required for some objects.

The player's final score reflects how effectively they manage the available resources while minimizing unnecessary waste.

---

# 8. Winning / Losing / Game Objectives

The main objective is to process objects while minimizing unnecessary waste and maximizing the useful life of available resources.

Players receive points for:

* correctly sorting objects;
* correctly identifying repairable objects;
* successfully repairing objects;
* returning repaired objects to use;
* recovering useful materials;
* making appropriate decisions after quantum measurements.

Players lose points for:

* incorrectly sorting objects;
* unnecessarily discarding repairable objects;
* making incorrect repair decisions;
* wasting limited repair resources;
* incorrectly acting on diagnostic information.

Each level has a required score or performance threshold.

For example:

**100 points → unlock next level**

The exact scoring values will be adjusted during testing and balancing.

The overall objective is to complete all levels while achieving a high sustainability score and minimizing unnecessary waste.

# 10. Originality / Existing Games

The game combines familiar mechanics from sorting games, puzzle games, resource-management games, repair games, and quantum games.

However, **Circular Silo** combines these mechanics around the concept of a circular resource system.

Instead of simply asking:

> "Which recycling bin does this object belong in?"

the game asks:

> **"What is the best next use for this object?"**

An object may therefore follow different paths:

```text
Object
  ↓
Reuse
```

or

```text
Object
  ↓
Repair
  ↓
Reuse
```

or

```text
Object
  ↓
Material Recovery
  ↓
Recycle
```

or, when other options are no longer viable:

```text
Object
  ↓
Waste
```

The repair system adds another gameplay layer after sorting. Correctly identifying an object as repairable is not enough; the player must also successfully diagnose and repair it.

The quantum contribution adds another layer for certain advanced electronic objects. Players use quantum measurement to determine an initially unknown component state and then make a resource-management decision based on the result.

The combination of:

**post-apocalyptic resource scarcity + circular economy + repair gameplay + quantum measurement**

forms the central identity of the project.

# 11. Anything Else

The project will initially focus on creating a small but complete gameplay loop rather than attempting to create a large-scale game.

The core gameplay loop will be:

**Find → Inspect → Measure if necessary → Sort → Repair/Recycle → Reuse → Score**

The initial implementation will contain a limited number of object types and repair scenarios.

Additional objects, difficulty levels, repair scenarios, narrative elements, and visual features may be added if time permits.

The project will prioritize demonstrating the relationship between **resource scarcity, repair, reuse, recycling, circular economy, and quantum measurement** rather than creating a large amount of content.

The Silo setting also provides a narrative reason for resource scarcity and makes the consequences of waste meaningful within the game world.

The final game is intended to give players both a practical understanding of responsible consumption and production and a simple, interactive introduction to the concept of quantum measurement.
